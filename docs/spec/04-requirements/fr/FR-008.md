id: FR-008
title: "Draft the outline from the request and the material"
---

## Requirement

- **Statement:** The system drafts the outline proposal from the request, the material and the answers, and can add relevant general knowledge. The proposal plans slides that fit the stated topic, purpose, audience and emphasis, and keeps the meaning of the material. It follows instructions in the material on the content or on how to build the slides, within the request. It writes the outline in the language the user asks for. For a question the user skipped, it says what it assumed instead.
- **Status:** confirmed
- **Source:** use case flow, and owner decision, 2026-10-12: general knowledge can complete the outline; the meaning of the material is kept; owner decision, 2026-10-13: the agent can use instructions in an attachment on the content or on how to build the slides, within the request, and they give it no access of its own; owner decision, 2026-10-13: DeckAgent writes deck content in the language the user asks for
- **Updated at:** 13-10-2026

## Acceptance conditions

### AC-1 · Topic and purpose follow the request

- **Given:** A chat with no deck. The request is "a talk to convince our board to extend the pilot, from these notes". The notes describe a pilot project and its results.
- **When:** The system shows the outline proposal.
- **Then:**
  - The planned slides are about the pilot project.
  - The sequence of slides builds a case for extending the pilot.
- **Check:** judged

### AC-2 · Stated audience shapes the outline

- **Given:** A chat with no deck. The request is "a short talk for new nurses on hand hygiene", with no material.
- **When:** The system shows the outline proposal.
- **Then:**
  - The planned content and explanations suit people new to nursing work, for example by explaining basic terms.
  - The planned slides do not rely on specialist detail without explaining it.
- **Check:** judged

### AC-3 · Stated emphasis shapes the outline

- **Given:** The state of AC-1. The request adds "focus on the cost results".
- **When:** The system shows the outline proposal.
- **Then:**
  - The cost results stand out through their order, their detail or the space the proposal gives them.
  - A reader of the proposal can tell that the talk focuses on the cost results.
- **Check:** judged

### AC-4 · Requested number of slides

- **Given:** The state of AC-1. The request adds "five slides".
- **When:** The system shows the outline proposal.
- **Then:**
  - The proposal plans five slides.
- **Check:** deterministic
- **Status:** proposed default
- **Source:** proposed
- **Why:** The use cases say that the outline follows the request, but not that a stated number of slides is exact. An exact count makes instruction fidelity testable. Trade-off: the system cannot add a slide it thinks the talk needs, such as a title slide, without the user asking.

### AC-5 · Relevant material is used

- **Given:** A chat with no deck. The request is "a talk on our pilot results, from these notes". The notes give three pilot findings: fewer errors, faster handover and lower cost. They also mention the date of a staff party.
- **When:** The system shows the outline proposal.
- **Then:**
  - The planned slides use the three pilot findings.
- **Check:** judged

### AC-6 · Figures keep the meaning of the material

- **Given:** The state of AC-5. The notes say that errors fell from 40 to 30 a month.
- **When:** The system shows the outline proposal, and it gives a figure for the fall in errors.
- **Then:**
  - Each figure for the fall follows from the notes. Examples are "from 40 to 30 a month", "10 fewer a month" or "a 25% fall".
  - The proposal gives no figure for the fall that does not follow from the notes, such as "a 30% fall".
- **Check:** judged

### AC-7 · Answers shape the outline

- **Given:** A chat with no deck. The request is "a talk on our pilot". The system asked who the audience is, and the user answered "hospital board members".
- **When:** The system shows the outline proposal.
- **Then:**
  - The planned slides address hospital board members, for example by leading with results and costs.
- **Check:** judged

### AC-8 · Skipped question leads to a stated assumption

- **Given:** A chat with no deck. The request is "a talk on our pilot". The system asked who the audience is, and the user skipped the question.
- **When:** The system shows the outline proposal.
- **Then:**
  - The message with the outline says which audience the system assumed.
  - The planned slides fit that stated audience.
- **Check:** judged

### AC-9 · Material only, when the user asks

- **Given:** A chat with no deck. The request is "a talk on our pilot, use only these notes". The notes describe the pilot and its results.
- **When:** The system shows the outline proposal.
- **Then:**
  - The planned slides state no fact that the notes do not give.
- **Check:** judged

### AC-10 · Instructions in the material

- **Given:** A chat with no deck. The request "make a deck from my brief" has the attachment "brief.docx". The brief asks for five slides and for a slide on risks.
- **When:** The system shows the outline proposal.
- **Then:**
  - The outline plans five slides.
  - One planned slide is about risks.
- **Check:** judged

### AC-11 · The language the user asks for

- **Given:** A chat with no deck. The request is "a talk on our pilot, in Vietnamese". The notes the user gave are in English.
- **When:** The system shows the outline proposal.
- **Then:**
  - The titles and the points of the outline are in Vietnamese.
- **Check:** judged

### AC-12 · No language named

- **Given:** A chat with no deck. The request is written in Vietnamese and names no language. The notes the user gave are in English.
- **When:** The system shows the outline proposal.
- **Then:**
  - The titles and the points of the outline are in Vietnamese.
- **Check:** judged
- **Status:** proposed default
- **Source:** proposed
- **Why:** The owner decision follows the language the user asks for. A request that names none leaves the language open. The language of the request text is the closest sign of what the user wants. Trade-off: a user who wants the language of the material must say so.

## Notes

- The number of conditions is more than the reading guide of seven. They share one purpose: the outline follows what the user asked for and gave.
- Instructions in the material give the agent no access beyond NFR-003.
- An instruction in the material that conflicts with the request leaves the intent unclear. FR-020 covers the question when the meanings change the result a lot.
- Material that the system can use only in part is covered by FR-067.
