## Who

People with a permanent visual limitation who prepare material for others, work with assistive technology, and cannot check visual results by looking. This segment is deliberately about *using* the product as well as about the *output*. [assumed]

## Needs

| ID | Goal (user's words) | Situation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| N-1 | "Prepare my lecture slides independently, without asking a sighted colleague to check them." | I prepare lectures each term. Visual layout is something I cannot judge by looking. | [assumed] |
| N-2 | "Know what the tool is doing at every moment." | Long operations run and finish with no sound or text I can read, so I cannot tell whether anything happened. | [assumed] |
| N-3 | "Be sure my slides work for students who rely on assistive technology." | Some of my students use screen readers or need large text and high contrast. | [assumed] |
| N-4 | "Reuse last term's lecture with updated content." | Courses repeat each year with changes. | [assumed] |
| N-5 | "Work at my own pace without being timed out." | Using assistive technology is slower and I stop mid-task. | [assumed] |

## Knowledge

| Kind | Level | In practice | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Domain (the topic they present) | Expert | Knows the subject deeply. | [assumed] |
| Presentation craft | Working | Understands structure (headings, reading order, alt text) well, because assistive technology depends on it; does not judge visual polish. | [assumed] |
| AI / tool literacy | Working | Comfortable with assistive technology and text-based tools; wary of tools that rely on mouse or visual cues. | [assumed] |
| Language of use (what they write requests in) | Expert | Writes requests fluently. | [assumed] |
| Language of the deck and its audience | Expert | Teaches in the same language. | [assumed] |

## Context of use

1. Works at a desktop with a screen reader and screen magnification, mostly keyboard only. [assumed]
2. Source is the previous term's lecture notes. [assumed]
3. Presents live in a lecture hall; slides must work for students with and without assistive technology. [assumed]
4. Often pauses and returns to a task, because assistive tools make multi-step work slower. [assumed]

## Pain points and workarounds today

1. Asks a sighted colleague to check slide layout and reading order. [assumed]
2. Avoids tools whose controls cannot be reached with the keyboard or announced by the screen reader. [assumed]
3. Finds that automatically generated slides have no text alternatives for figures and a confusing reading order. [assumed]

## Accessibility and situational limits

| Type | Limit | Effect on use | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Permanent (user) | Low vision; relies on screen reader and magnification | Whole interface must be usable without seeing it or with strong magnification | [assumed] |
| Permanent (audience) | Some students rely on assistive technology or need large text and high contrast | Output must be accessible by construction, not only by appearance | [assumed] |

## Special-case hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Affects | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| H-1 | Every step from asking to delivery must work by keyboard and be announced by a screen reader, including long-running operations and their results. | Accessible interface; progress announcements | [assumed] |
| H-2 | The user cannot verify a visual preview by looking. The product would have to let the user verify structure (reading order, headings, text alternatives, contrast) through text. | Accessible verification; preview alternatives | [assumed] |
| H-3 | Generated slides must carry text alternatives for figures, a logical reading order and sufficient contrast without being asked. | Accessible output by default | [assumed] |
| H-4 | At high magnification (for example 400%), the interface must remain usable without horizontal scrolling or hidden controls. | Responsive layout; zoom | [assumed] |
| H-5 | Users on assistive technology work more slowly and pause. The product would have to avoid timeouts that lose work or force starting over. | Session handling; time limits | [assumed] |

## Open questions

1. Which assistive technologies (screen readers, magnifiers, voice control) matter most for the intended users? Resolved by: team research with accessibility practitioners.
2. Which accessibility standard should the output meet, and who decides? Resolved by: team decision with evidence from research.
