## Who

People who are new to a topic, a job or a field and need to understand it quickly, using material they were given but cannot yet judge. The deck is a study aid for themselves, not something they present. [assumed]

## Needs

| ID | Goal (user's words) | Situation | Use cases | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| N-1 | "Understand this unfamiliar area well enough to follow meetings by next week." | I started a new job. The handbook is long and full of terms I do not know. | UC-001 | [assumed] |
| N-2 | "Know which parts matter most and which can wait." | I cannot tell what is central and what is detail because I do not know the field. | none yet | [assumed] |
| N-3 | "Find out where I have misunderstood." | I may be learning something wrong and have no way to notice. | none yet | [assumed] |
| N-4 | "Have unfamiliar terms explained where I meet them." | The material uses acronyms and jargon that colleagues take for granted. | none yet | [assumed] |
| N-5 | "Avoid asking colleagues basic questions all day." | I am new and do not want to look unprepared. | none yet | [assumed] |
| N-6 | "Come back and add to my notes as I learn more." | My understanding grows over weeks and my first picture will change. | none yet | [assumed] |

## Context of use

1. Works on a laptop at work or home, mostly in the first weeks of a new role. [assumed]
2. Source is an onboarding handbook or a few internal documents. [assumed]
3. The only reader is the user; there is no review and no deadline beyond "soon". [assumed]
4. Internal material may have sharing limits that the user has not checked (see open questions). [assumed]

## Pain points and workarounds today

1. Reads the handbook straight through and forgets most of it. [assumed]
2. Keeps handwritten or typed notes, with no structure and no way to see how topics relate. [assumed]
3. Holds back questions to avoid bothering colleagues, so misunderstandings last. [assumed]

## Special-case hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Affects | Handled by | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H-1 | The user cannot judge correctness. The product would have to show where each statement comes from and distinguish source content from content it added, so the user does not learn errors. | Source tracing; marking added content | none yet | [assumed] |
| H-2 | The deck is for study, not for presenting. The product would have to establish the purpose early and avoid imposing presentation conventions (sparse slides, speaker-oriented structure) when the user wants dense, recall-friendly material. | Clarifying purpose; content density and structure | none yet | [assumed] |
| H-3 | Jargon and acronyms in the source are opaque to the user. The product would have to explain or flag unfamiliar terms instead of repeating them. | Handling unknown terms; explanation | none yet | [assumed] |
| H-4 | The user does not know what to ask. The product would have to surface what the source assumes or leaves out, not only what it says. | Surfacing gaps; clarifying questions | none yet | [assumed] |
| H-5 | Understanding grows over weeks. The user would need to return and extend the same deck without losing earlier work or structure. | Reopening and extending a deck | none yet | [assumed] |

## Open questions

1. Does internal onboarding material usually carry restrictions on being uploaded to outside services? Resolved by: team interviews with new employees.
2. Do newcomers want a deck, or would they prefer another form of study aid? Resolved by: team interviews (and a check against what the baseline products offer).

## Notes

1. Distinct from USR-001 on purpose, domain knowledge, stakes and audience (the user alone); distinct from USR-004 on purpose and domain knowledge.
2. Whether a *slide deck* is the right format for this need is itself uncertain (open question 2). The segment is kept because the need (learn an unfamiliar topic from provided material) surfaces use cases regardless of format.
