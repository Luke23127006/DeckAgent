## Who

- Final-year university students present and defend a thesis to a committee.
- They present in a language that is not their first.
- They have little time, no income to spend on tools, and no experience building slides.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Needs

### N-1 · Fit the thesis into 20 minutes

- **Goal:** "Explain my whole thesis to the committee in 20 minutes without dropping the results that matter."
- **Situation:** The defense is in three days. The thesis is about 80 pages. I know the content but not how to shrink it.
- **Use cases:** UC-001
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### N-2 · Answer any question on the slides

- **Goal:** "Be able to answer any question about any number or claim on my slides."
- **Situation:** Committee members probe results. A wrong or unexplained number could cost me the grade.
- **Use cases:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### N-3 · Sound clear in English

- **Goal:** "Sound clear and confident in English even though it is not my first language."
- **Situation:** I present in English. I read slides aloud and stumble on long or unfamiliar words.
- **Use cases:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### N-4 · Finish without paying

- **Goal:** "Finish without paying anything."
- **Situation:** I am a student with no income and can only use what is free.
- **Use cases:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### N-5 · Slides that open on any computer

- **Goal:** "Be sure my slides will look right and open on the defense room's computer."
- **Situation:** The department supplies that machine. I cannot test on it beforehand.
- **Use cases:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Knowledge

- **Domain (the topic they present):** Expert. Knows the thesis topic in depth and will notice wrong claims. Does not know what to cut.
- **Presentation craft:** Basic. Has made a few class slides. Cannot judge hierarchy or how much text is too much.
- **AI / tool literacy:** Working. Uses chat assistants for study and knows prompting basics. May over-trust the output.
- **Language of use (what they write requests in):** Expert. Writes requests fluently in the first language.
- **Language of the deck and its audience:** Working. Reads and writes English well enough for a defense but speaks it with effort.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Context of use

- Works on a laptop, at home or in the library.
- Works in the evenings over two to three days.
- Presents live from a computer supplied by the department.
- Sends a copy to the advisor beforehand.
- The advisor reviews informally. There is no formal approval step.
- Has no money for tools, so free allowances are the only option.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Pain points and workarounds today

- Copies thesis passages onto slides and ends up with text-heavy slides.
- Asks a chat assistant for slide text, pastes it into a blank presentation tool, and fixes the layout by hand.
- Rewrites sentences repeatedly to make them easy to say aloud.
- Reaches the free limit of a tool close to the deadline.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Accessibility and situational limits

### Permanent

- **Limit:** None identified: no limit assumed for this segment.
- **Effect on use:** None.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### Situational (audience)

- **Limit:** A projector in a bright defense room washes out low-contrast slides.
- **Effect on use:** Slides must stay readable under poor display conditions.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Special-case hypotheses

### H-1 · Long document, fixed talk length

- **Situation:** The source is a long document, about 80 pages with figures and tables. The talk has a fixed length, about 20 minutes.
- **Product would have to:** Accept long input and relate the number of slides to speaking time.
- **Affects:** Create from a document; input limits
- **Handled by:** UC-003.A3 (long input only)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-2 · Every number gets questioned

- **Situation:** Every number on a slide will be questioned.
- **Product would have to:** Trace each figure back to the thesis. Make anything the system adds distinguishable from thesis content.
- **Affects:** Source fidelity; marking added content
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-3 · Request language differs from deck language

- **Situation:** The user thinks in a first language but presents in English. The language of the request may differ from the language of the deck.
- **Product would have to:** Use wording simple enough to say aloud.
- **Affects:** Language choice; style and reading level
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-4 · Unknown defense computer

- **Situation:** The defense computer is unknown.
- **Product would have to:** Deliver a file that displays correctly where the user's fonts and software are missing.
- **Affects:** Output portability; fonts
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-5 · Usage limit the night before

- **Situation:** Free-only users on a deadline may reach a usage limit the night before.
- **Product would have to:** Keep existing work intact. Say plainly what remains and when.
- **Affects:** Usage limits; error behavior
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
