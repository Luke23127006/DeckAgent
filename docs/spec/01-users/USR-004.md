## Who

Analysts and research staff who regularly turn data and large bodies of source material into reports that decision makers read before meeting, often for readers in several languages. [assumed]

## Needs

| ID | Goal (user's words) | Situation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| N-1 | "Show the committee what the data says without distorting it." | Decisions follow from these figures. A wrong axis, unit or rounding would mislead them. | [assumed] |
| N-2 | "Pull the key findings from dozens of sources without missing the ones that matter." | About 40 papers and a large spreadsheet feed each quarter's briefing. | [assumed] |
| N-3 | "Point to where every claim comes from." | Committee members ask for the source of any statement. | [assumed] |
| N-4 | "Produce next quarter's briefing from new data without rebuilding it." | The structure stays the same each quarter; only the numbers and some findings change. | [assumed] |
| N-5 | "Give readers in different languages the same numbers and meaning." | Members read in several languages and compare versions across them. | [assumed] |

## Knowledge

| Kind | Level | In practice | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Domain (the topic they present) | Expert | Knows the field and sources deeply. Will notice a misread result immediately. | [assumed] |
| Presentation craft | Basic | Can build plain charts and tables. Does not design visually. | [assumed] |
| AI / tool literacy | Working | Uses AI assistants for summarizing and knows to check them, but has limited feel for how much input they can handle. | [assumed] |
| Language of use (what they write requests in) | Expert | Writes requests fluently in a professional working language. | [assumed] |
| Language of the deck and its audience | Working | Confident in the main language of the deck; less sure how wording lands for readers of other languages. | [assumed] |

## Context of use

1. Works at a desktop, over days each quarter, alone or with a reviewing colleague. [assumed]
2. Source material is a large spreadsheet and many papers, far longer than a normal prompt. [assumed]
3. The briefing is sent to the committee to read before the meeting, not presented slide by slide. [assumed]
4. Findings may be unpublished; the segment has not stated a data-handling rule (see open questions). [assumed]

## Pain points and workarounds today

1. Summarizes papers one by one into a notes file, then copies the notes into slides. [assumed]
2. Retypes numbers from the spreadsheet into charts and loses track of which version is current. [assumed]
3. Rebuilds the briefing from scratch each quarter because the old file is tied to last quarter's data. [assumed]
4. Keeps separate files per language and finds numbers drift apart between them. [assumed]

## Special-case hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Affects | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| H-1 | Figures, units, rounding and labels must match the source tables exactly. The product would have to treat numbers as data to be carried over exactly, not as text to be rewritten. | Data fidelity; charts; number handling | [assumed] |
| H-2 | Source material (dozens of papers and a large spreadsheet) exceeds any single input. The product would have to handle many or large inputs and say plainly what it read, what it skipped and why. | Input volume and limits; coverage reporting | [assumed] |
| H-3 | Committee members ask where a claim came from. Each claim would have to link back to a source passage. | Source tracing; citation | [assumed] |
| H-4 | The same briefing is rebuilt each quarter. The user would need to update numbers and findings while keeping structure and layout. | Reusing a previous deck; updating data | [assumed] |
| H-5 | Readers compare versions in several languages. Numbers, layout and meaning would have to stay consistent across language versions. | Multi-language versions; consistency | [assumed] |
| H-6 | Tables with many columns cannot all fit on a slide. The product would have to decide what to show, split or leave to an appendix, and let the user override. | Dense tables; layout decisions | [assumed] |

## Open questions

1. Are unpublished findings subject to data-handling rules that change what can be uploaded? Resolved by: team interviews with analysts.
2. How large are typical source sets in practice (number of documents, rows)? Resolved by: team interviews.
