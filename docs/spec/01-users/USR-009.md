## Who

- Founders and entrepreneurs must turn an early, still-forming idea into a story others can judge.
- They often have no designer, no team and no experience of what investors expect.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Needs

### N-1 · Turn a concept into a story

- **Goal:** "Turn my vague concept into a story investors can follow."
- **Situation:** I know what I want to build but have never written it down in order.
- **Use cases:** UC-001
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### N-2 · Find holes in my idea

- **Goal:** "Find the holes in my idea before the investors do."
- **Situation:** I have a meeting in two weeks and no one to challenge me.
- **Use cases:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### N-3 · Look credible without a designer

- **Goal:** "Look credible without a designer or a budget."
- **Situation:** Investors judge quickly, and I cannot pay for design.
- **Use cases:** UC-001
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### N-4 · Change the story quickly

- **Goal:** "Change the story quickly as I get feedback."
- **Situation:** Advisors and early contacts keep giving me different advice.
- **Use cases:** UC-002
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### N-5 · One version to read, one to present

- **Goal:** "Have a version investors can read on their own and a version I can present."
- **Situation:** They read the deck before the meeting, and I present it in the meeting.
- **Use cases:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### N-6 · Claim only what I can back up

- **Goal:** "Not claim anything I cannot back up."
- **Situation:** A made-up market number can end the meeting.
- **Use cases:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Knowledge

- **Domain (the topic they present):** Working. Knows their own idea well in outline but not the market or the numbers.
- **Presentation craft:** Basic. Has seen good decks but has not built one.
- **AI / tool literacy:** Working. Already uses AI tools to explore ideas and expects them to help think.
- **Language of use (what they write requests in):** Expert. Writes fluently.
- **Language of the deck and its audience:** Expert. Pitches in a shared language.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Context of use

- Works at a laptop, mostly alone or with a co-founder.
- Works over a couple of weeks.
- Starts with no document: a concept in their head and perhaps a few scribbled notes.
- The deck is emailed to investors beforehand and presented live.
- Has tight money. Pays for tools only if clearly worth it.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Pain points and workarounds today

- Stares at a blank deck and does not know the expected structure.
- Copies another startup's deck and rewrites it, which fits poorly.
- Fills gaps with plausible-sounding numbers found online and later cannot source them.
- Asks friends for feedback and gets contradictory advice.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Special-case hypotheses

### H-1 · No source document

- **Situation:** There is no source document.
- **Product would have to:** Work from a conversation and draw out the idea through questions, not only transform existing material.
- **Affects:** Starting from an idea; clarifying questions
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-2 · Vague, contradictory input

- **Situation:** The input is vague and may contradict itself.
- **Product would have to:** Say what it assumed. Mark what is the founder's claim and what it filled in.
- **Affects:** Marking assumptions; vague input
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-3 · Made-up figures are dangerous

- **Situation:** A made-up figure is dangerous for credibility.
- **Product would have to:** Avoid inventing numbers, or mark them clearly as placeholders.
- **Affects:** Source fidelity; placeholders
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-4 · User does not know what investors expect

- **Situation:** The user does not know what investors expect.
- **Product would have to:** Offer a conventional structure while leaving room to depart from it.
- **Affects:** Structure guidance
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-5 · Story changes after feedback

- **Situation:** The story changes after each round of feedback.
- **Product would have to:** Let the user keep earlier versions and compare them.
- **Affects:** Version history; comparison
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-6 · One content, two uses

- **Situation:** The same content serves a reader (email) and a live pitch.
- **Product would have to:** Serve both without the user maintaining two decks.
- **Affects:** Output variants; text density
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-7 · Idea confidentiality

- **Situation:** An unprotected idea shared with an outside service is a worry for the founder.
- **Product would have to:** State clearly where the idea goes and who can see it.
- **Affects:** Data disclosure
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
