## Who

- People with a permanent visual limitation prepare material for others.
- They work with assistive technology and cannot check visual results by looking.
- This segment is deliberately about *using* the product, as well as about the *output*.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Needs

### N-1 · Prepare slides independently

- **Goal:** "Prepare my lecture slides independently, without asking a sighted colleague to check them."
- **Situation:** I prepare lectures each term. Visual layout is something I cannot judge by looking.
- **Use cases:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026

### N-2 · Know what the tool is doing

- **Goal:** "Know what the tool is doing at every moment."
- **Situation:** Long operations run and finish with no sound or text I can read. So I cannot tell whether anything happened.
- **Use cases:** UC-003 (status in words only; announcing it to assistive technology is an NFR)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 10-10-2026

### N-3 · Slides that work with assistive technology

- **Goal:** "Be sure my slides work for students who rely on assistive technology."
- **Situation:** Some of my students use screen readers or need large text and high contrast.
- **Use cases:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026

### N-4 · Reuse last term's lecture

- **Goal:** "Reuse last term's lecture with updated content."
- **Situation:** Courses repeat each year with changes.
- **Use cases:** UC-001 (A29, structure only)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 09-10-2026

### N-5 · No time-outs

- **Goal:** "Work at my own pace without being timed out."
- **Situation:** Using assistive technology is slower. I stop mid-task.
- **Use cases:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026

## Knowledge

- **Domain (the topic they present):** Expert. Knows the subject deeply.
- **Presentation craft:** Working. Understands structure (headings, reading order, alt text) well, because assistive technology depends on it. Does not judge visual polish.
- **AI / tool literacy:** Working. Is comfortable with assistive technology and text-based tools. Is wary of tools that rely on a mouse or visual cues.
- **Language of use (what they write requests in):** Expert. Writes requests fluently.
- **Language of the deck and its audience:** Expert. Teaches in the same language.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Context of use

- Works at a desktop with a screen reader and screen magnification.
- Works mostly by keyboard only.
- The source is the previous term's lecture notes.
- Presents live in a lecture hall.
- The slides must work for students with and without assistive technology.
- Often pauses and returns to a task, because assistive tools make multi-step work slower.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Pain points and workarounds today

- Asks a sighted colleague to check slide layout and reading order.
- Avoids tools whose controls cannot be reached with the keyboard or announced by the screen reader.
- Finds that automatically generated slides have no text alternatives for figures and a confusing reading order.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Accessibility and situational limits

### Permanent (user)

- **Limit:** Low vision. Relies on a screen reader and magnification.
- **Effect on use:** The whole interface must be usable without seeing it, or with strong magnification.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026

### Permanent (audience)

- **Limit:** Some students rely on assistive technology or need large text and high contrast.
- **Effect on use:** Output must be accessible by construction, not only by appearance.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026

## Special-case hypotheses

### H-1 · Keyboard and screen reader for every step

- **Situation:** The user works by keyboard and screen reader.
- **Product would have to:** Make every step from asking to delivery work by keyboard and be announced by a screen reader. This includes long-running operations and their results.
- **Affects:** Accessible interface; progress announcements
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026

### H-2 · Cannot verify a preview by looking

- **Situation:** The user cannot verify a visual preview by looking.
- **Product would have to:** Let the user verify structure through text: reading order, headings, text alternatives and contrast.
- **Affects:** Accessible verification; preview alternatives
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026

### H-3 · Accessible output by default

- **Situation:** The user cannot check slides by looking, and some students rely on assistive technology.
- **Product would have to:** Give generated slides text alternatives for figures, a logical reading order and sufficient contrast, without being asked.
- **Affects:** Accessible output by default
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026

### H-4 · High magnification

- **Situation:** The user works at high magnification, for example 400%.
- **Product would have to:** Keep the interface usable without horizontal scrolling or hidden controls.
- **Affects:** Responsive layout; zoom
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026

### H-5 · Slower pace and pauses

- **Situation:** Users on assistive technology work more slowly and pause.
- **Product would have to:** Avoid time-outs that lose work or force starting over.
- **Affects:** Sessions; time limits
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026
