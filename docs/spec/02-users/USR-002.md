## Who

- Managers or senior staff must win a client with a deck within hours.
- They use their company's approved look and rules.
- They know the business well but rarely build slides.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Needs

### N-1 · Client-ready deck by this afternoon

- **Goal:** "Have a client-ready deck for the pitch this afternoon."
- **Situation:** The client moved the meeting up. I have about 90 minutes and last quarter's deck for another client.
- **Use cases:** UC-001 (A29, structure only)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### N-2 · Keep the company look and wording

- **Goal:** "Keep the company's look and approved wording intact."
- **Situation:** Marketing and legal approved the wording. Off-brand slides make me look bad in front of the client.
- **Use cases:** UC-001 (look only, A5), UC-002 (unnamed slides only)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### N-3 · Keep client data inside company rules

- **Goal:** "Keep client and pricing information inside what my company allows."
- **Situation:** The deck holds confidential figures. Company policy limits where data may go.
- **Use cases:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### N-4 · Quick sign-off with visible changes

- **Goal:** "Get my director's sign-off quickly and show exactly what changed."
- **Situation:** The director reviews every client deck before it goes out. The director has little time to read all of it.
- **Use cases:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### N-5 · Present live, send safely

- **Goal:** "Present live and send the same deck afterwards without exposing what is meant for me only."
- **Situation:** I have talking notes and fallback prices that I use in the room. I do not want the client to keep them.
- **Use cases:** UC-005.A2 (presenter notes only)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Knowledge

- **Domain (the topic they present):** Expert. Knows the product, market and client. Will spot any error in the business content.
- **Presentation craft:** Basic. Works within the company look. Cannot judge visual quality beyond "looks like ours".
- **AI / tool literacy:** Basic. Has tried a chat assistant a few times. Is unsure what it can and cannot do, and is wary of mistakes.
- **Language of use (what they write requests in):** Expert. Writes requests in the first language without difficulty.
- **Language of the deck and its audience:** Expert. Presents in the first language to a client who shares it.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Context of use

- Works at a desktop or laptop in the office.
- Has a few hours or less.
- Presents the deck live, then emails it to the client.
- A director reviews before release. Legal or marketing may also need to approve.
- The company pays for tools but restricts where client data may be sent.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Pain points and workarounds today

- Duplicates last quarter's deck and edits by hand. Misses leftover names or figures from the previous client.
- Waits for a colleague or agency to fix formatting.
- Avoids pasting client data into public chat tools because of policy. Does the content work by hand.
- Sends reviewers the whole file with no list of what changed.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Special-case hypotheses

### H-1 · Leftover content from a previous client

- **Situation:** A reused deck still contains the previous client's names and figures.
- **Product would have to:** Help surface stale or foreign content when an existing deck is reused.
- **Affects:** Working from an existing deck; content checks
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-2 · Brand and approved text must not change

- **Situation:** Approved brand elements and wording must not change when the deck is revised.
- **Product would have to:** Respect locked brand elements and approved text when a change is made.
- **Affects:** Scoped changes; brand constraints
- **Handled by:** UC-002.S3 (unnamed slides only)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-3 · Reviewer with little time

- **Situation:** The director reviews with little time.
- **Product would have to:** Show the reviewer what changed since the last approved version. Let the reviewer approve the deck or send it back.
- **Affects:** Version history; sharing for review
- **Handled by:** rejected: the product has no sharing for review and no approval between people
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-4 · Company policy limits where data goes

- **Situation:** Company policy limits where client data may go.
- **Product would have to:** Tell the user, before upload, where data is sent and kept, and whether it is used to train models.
- **Affects:** Data disclosure; retention; deletion
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-5 · Same deck, live and emailed

- **Situation:** The same deck is presented live and emailed.
- **Product would have to:** Keep presenter-only content (notes, fallback prices) out of the shared file.
- **Affects:** Output variants; presenter-only content
- **Handled by:** UC-005.A2 (presenter notes only)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

### H-6 · Short window, no long waits

- **Situation:** A 90-minute window cannot absorb long waits.
- **Product would have to:** Give a usable partial result early. Give a safe way to stop.
- **Affects:** Progress; stopping; partial results
- **Handled by:** UC-001.S5 (partial results only)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
