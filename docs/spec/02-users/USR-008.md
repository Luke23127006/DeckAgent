## Who

- People are new to a topic, a job or a field and need to understand it quickly.
- They use material they were given but cannot yet judge.
- The deck is a study aid for themselves. They do not present it.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Knowledge

- **Domain (the topic they present):** None. Cannot judge whether generated content about the topic is correct or complete.
- **Presentation craft:** Basic. Does not need polish, because the deck is for personal use.
- **AI / tool literacy:** Basic. Has tried an AI assistant. May trust it too much because of the missing domain knowledge.
- **Language of use (what they write requests in):** Expert. Writes fluently.
- **Language of the deck and its audience:** Expert. The only reader is the user.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Context of use

- Works on a laptop at work or home.
- Works mostly in the first weeks of a new role.
- The source is an onboarding handbook or a few internal documents.
- The only reader is the user.
- There is no review and no deadline beyond "soon".
- Internal material may have sharing limits that the user has not checked (see open questions).
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Pain points and workarounds today

- Reads the handbook straight through and forgets most of it.
- Keeps handwritten or typed notes, with no structure and no way to see how topics relate.
- Holds back questions to avoid bothering colleagues, so misunderstandings last.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Special-case hypotheses

### H-1 · User cannot judge correctness

- **Situation:** The user cannot judge correctness.
- **Product would have to:** Show where each statement comes from. Distinguish source content from content it added, so the user does not learn errors.
- **Affects:** Source tracing; marking added content
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026

### H-2 · Study aid, not a presentation

- **Situation:** The deck is for study, not for presenting.
- **Product would have to:** Establish the purpose early. Avoid imposing presentation conventions (sparse slides, speaker-oriented structure) when the user wants dense, recall-friendly material.
- **Affects:** Clarifying purpose; content density and structure
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026

### H-3 · Opaque jargon

- **Situation:** Jargon and acronyms in the source are opaque to the user.
- **Product would have to:** Explain or flag unfamiliar terms instead of repeating them.
- **Affects:** Unknown terms; explanation
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026

### H-4 · User does not know what to ask

- **Situation:** The user does not know what to ask.
- **Product would have to:** Surface what the source assumes or leaves out, not only what it says.
- **Affects:** Surfacing gaps; clarifying questions
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026

### H-5 · Understanding grows over weeks

- **Situation:** Understanding grows over weeks.
- **Product would have to:** Let the user return and extend the same deck without losing earlier work or structure.
- **Affects:** Reopening and extending a deck
- **Handled by:** none yet
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 08-10-2026
