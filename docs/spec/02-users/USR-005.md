## Who

- Professional designers and consultants build decks for clients as a service.
- They judge visual quality closely and are fluent with AI tools.
- They answer to clients' brand rules and approval rounds.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Needs

### N-1 · Try several directions fast

- **Goal:** "Try several visual directions quickly before committing to one."
- **Situation:** A client pitch is due Friday and the client's taste is unknown.
- **Use cases:** UC-001 (looks before the first draft only)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 10-10-2026

### N-2 · Keep hand-set details

- **Goal:** "Keep every detail I set by hand when something else changes."
- **Situation:** I fine-tune spacing and type. A broad change that resets them wastes my afternoon.
- **Use cases:** UC-002 (unnamed slides only)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 09-10-2026

### N-3 · Match the client brand book

- **Goal:** "Match the client's brand book exactly."
- **Situation:** The client's brand guide defines colors, fonts and spacing. The client notices deviations.
- **Use cases:** UC-001 (A5), UC-010 (rules set up, not checked exactly)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 10-10-2026

### N-4 · Client review by version

- **Goal:** "Let the client review and comment, and know which version they commented on."
- **Situation:** The client gives feedback in rounds. I need to tell revision 2 from revision 3.
- **Use cases:** UC-008 (earlier states only)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 10-10-2026

### N-5 · Editable handover

- **Goal:** "Hand over a deck the client's team can keep editing."
- **Situation:** After delivery the client's own staff maintain the deck in their tools.
- **Use cases:** UC-004 (editable file)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 10-10-2026

## Knowledge

- **Domain (the topic they present):** Working. Learns each client's industry enough to present it well. Relies on the client for facts.
- **Presentation craft:** Expert. Judges layout, hierarchy and typography closely. Notices small flaws and expects control over them.
- **AI / tool literacy:** Expert. Writes precise instructions, iterates fast and knows what tools do badly. Expects shortcuts and advanced options.
- **Language of use (what they write requests in):** Expert. Writes requests fluently in a working language.
- **Language of the deck and its audience:** Expert. Works in the client's language.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Context of use

- Works at a desktop.
- Works on several client projects at once.
- Starts from the client's notes and a messy draft deck. Then explores directions before settling on one.
- Shares drafts with clients through a link. Collects comments in rounds.
- Pays for tools personally. Expects them to fit a professional workflow.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Pain points and workarounds today

- Generated slides look generic, so rebuilds the layout by hand.
- Loses manual fine-tuning when asking an AI tool to revise something else.
- Sends clients exported files and loses track of which version each comment refers to.
- **Evidence:** [assumed]

## Special-case hypotheses

### H-1 · Hand-set details must survive

- **Situation:** Hand-set details (position, spacing, type) must survive later AI changes.
- **Product would have to:** Tell apart what the user fixed by hand from what is open to change. Respect the difference.
- **Affects:** Scoped changes; protecting manual edits
- **Handled by:** UC-002.S3 (unnamed slides only)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 09-10-2026

### H-2 · Many directions side by side

- **Situation:** A designer explores many directions and compares them.
- **Product would have to:** Produce alternatives side by side. Let the user pick or combine them.
- **Affects:** Alternatives; comparison
- **Handled by:** UC-001.A36 (one look chosen), UC-011.S4 (one variant at a time; no combining)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 10-10-2026

### H-3 · Exact brand book

- **Situation:** A client's brand book is exact, not approximate.
- **Product would have to:** Apply specific colors, fonts and spacing precisely. Flag deviations.
- **Affects:** Brand constraints; precision
- **Handled by:** UC-001.A5, UC-010.A4 (rules taken from a brand guide, deviations not flagged)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 10-10-2026

### H-4 · Client reviews a link

- **Situation:** Clients review a link, not a file.
- **Product would have to:** Allow comments. Show which version each comment belongs to.
- **Affects:** Sharing; comments; version identity
- **Handled by:** rejected: the product has no sharing by link and no comments between people
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 09-10-2026

### H-5 · Client team edits later

- **Situation:** The client's team edits the deck later in other tools.
- **Product would have to:** Deliver a file structured as a person would build it, not flattened.
- **Affects:** Output structure; editability
- **Handled by:** UC-004 (editable file)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 10-10-2026

### H-6 · No hidden decisions

- **Situation:** An expert will not tolerate fixed, hidden decisions.
- **Product would have to:** Make anything the system decides automatically visible and overridable.
- **Affects:** Transparency; overrides
- **Handled by:** UC-001.S6 (assumptions made visible only; no override)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 10-10-2026

### H-7 · Messy client input

- **Situation:** The client's notes are incomplete or contradict each other. The draft deck is inconsistent.
- **Product would have to:** Surface conflicts and gaps in the input instead of silently picking one.
- **Affects:** Messy input; clarifying questions
- **Handled by:** UC-001.A33, UC-001.A34 (first draft only)
- **Evidence:** [assumed]
- **Updated at:** 10-10-2026
